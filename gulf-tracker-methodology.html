Gulf States Vision 2030 & AI Investment Tracker — Methodology

A comparative framework for tracking US-China technology alignment across the Gulf and wider Middle East/South Asia region.

Gulf States Vision 2030 & AI Investment Tracker (internally, the Gulf AI & Tech-Bloc Alignment Tracker) is a comparative dashboard scoring 17 economies on a Net Alignment Score — US Integration Depth weighed against China Exposure Depth — built from 4 scored factors across two weighted axes, plus 2 more factors tracked as context. A Gulf state maximizing ties with both Washington and Beijing at once isn't "confused" — it's hedging, and that is exactly the pattern this tracker is built to surface rather than collapse into one misleadingly clean number. Designed as a research/portfolio project; not a forecasting tool, investment signal, or authoritative government assessment.

1. Purpose

Gulf states have spent the past several years hedging between two technology spheres at once — the UAE, for instance, holds the region's most US-favorable chip-export status while still running Huawei radio equipment on live 5G networks. That's not confusion; it's a deliberate dual-track strategy, and a single linear "which side is this country on" score would collapse exactly the signal worth seeing. This tracker is built to keep that signal visible: a quantitative Net Alignment Score paired with a sourced, chronological policy-event feed and per-country deep-dive briefs, so a number is never presented without the events that produced it.

This is a research/portfolio project, not a forecasting tool, investment signal, or authoritative government assessment. Where public data is too thin to responsibly support a number, that gap is shown explicitly as insufficient data rather than filled with an estimate presented as fact — the same principle the companion MENASA Risk Monitor applies to Iran's sanctions-driven data gaps.

2. Coverage

17 economies in total, added in three stages rather than all at once:

Group Countries
Gulf core (6)Saudi Arabia, United Arab Emirates, Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, Oman
Original comparators (2)Pakistan, Turkey — both navigating a similar, smaller-scale US/China balancing act
Regional additions (9)Israel, Egypt, Jordan, Iraq, Lebanon, Syria, Iran, Yemen, Afghanistan

The 9 regional additions started as a map-rendering fix, not an analytical decision: the Overview map's boundary data originally covered only the 8 tracked countries, so Turkey, the Gulf peninsula, and Pakistan rendered as three disconnected landmasses with the space between them left empty. A first pass added those 9 countries as unscored gray "geographic context only" filler purely so the map read as one contiguous region — then a direct follow-up decision turned that filler into 9 fully scored countries, researched and cited to the same standard as the original 8. The name "Gulf AI & Tech-Bloc Alignment Tracker" and the portfolio's "Gulf States" branding were kept as-is rather than renamed mid-project, worth knowing if "Gulf" reads as meaning strictly the 6 GCC states.

Two of the 17 needed real judgment calls worth flagging directly: Israel scores the highest Net Alignment in the entire set (zero Chinese telecom-vendor presence — Huawei/ZTE were excluded from its core networks in 2019 — plus a Dec 2025 multilateral chip-supply declaration), a genuinely different profile from every Gulf state in the dataset. Syria still scores 0 on export-control tier despite most of its broader sanctions being lifted in 2025, because a specific BIS guidance document (31 May 2026) confirmed the advanced-computing/AI-chip license requirement remained in force regardless — the research is current, that particular restriction just didn't move with the rest.

3. Framework

Each country is scored on two axes, not one blended number:

  • US Integration Depth (0–100) — a weighted average of three factors: US export-control access tier (40%), disclosed AI infrastructure investment (30%), and disclosed/under-development compute capacity (30%). Export-control tier is weighted heaviest because it's the structural gatekeeper — a country's formal BIS status determines what capital and hardware commitments are even legally possible before the other two factors can move.
  • China Exposure Depth (0–100) — currently a single factor: Chinese tech penetration, chiefly Huawei's telecom/cloud footprint. This is a disclosed, deliberate limitation (see Limitations below), not an oversight.

The two axes combine into one derived Net Alignment Score (0–100, 50 = neutral):

Net Alignment Score = 50 + (US Integration Depth − China Exposure Depth) / 2. Higher means more US-integrated relative to Chinese exposure; lower means more Chinese-exposed relative to US integration. 50 does not mean "inactive" — a country maxing out both axes and a country doing little on either can both land near 50, which is why the two sub-scores are always shown alongside the headline number, never in place of it.

Two more factors are tracked but deliberately excluded from the score itself — they measure state capacity, not bloc alignment, since a mature AI regulator or a diversified economy doesn't inherently signal leaning toward either side:

  • AI governance maturity — existence of a national AI strategy, a dedicated regulator, and binding sectoral rules.
  • Non-oil economic diversification — a World Bank-derived proxy (100 minus oil rents as % of GDP), the same approximation used in IMF Article IV coverage of GCC economies, since no single World Bank indicator cleanly exposes "non-oil GDP share" across this country set.

Why 6 factors and not 8–10: the original project brief allowed 6–8. Research confirmed all 6 are sourceable for at least the region's two AI leaders, but data depth drops off sharply for the smaller Gulf states and the original comparators — a methodologically sound 6-factor index (4 scored, 2 context) beat padding to 8–10 with factors that would sit mostly N/A outside Saudi Arabia and the UAE.

4. Normalization Method

The two dollar/megawatt factors — investment and compute capacity — are normalized with a fixed-ceiling log scale, not dataset-relative min-max: log10(x+1) / log10(ceiling+1) × 100, clipped to [0, 100]. With only 2–3 countries carrying any disclosed figure at all, min-max normalization would stretch a modest real gap — Saudi Arabia's $34.2bn in scored AI-investment deals against the UAE's $15.2bn — into an artificial 100-vs-0 spread, misrepresenting two countries that are both genuinely substantial investors.

Fixed ceilings keep scores stable as new deals are disclosed and make "what it would take to score 100" an explicit, documented choice rather than an artifact of whoever else happens to be in the dataset that month. Both ceilings are grounded in the data itself: the investment ceiling ($50bn) sits just above Saudi Arabia's current scored total, and the compute ceiling (6,000 MW) matches Saudi Arabia's own disclosed 2034 national target.

5. Ordinal Rubrics

The two curated 0–5 factors — US export-control access tier and Chinese tech penetration — are scored on a documented rubric rather than pulled from a single clean public number, because no such number exists at this granularity. These are analyst-desk judgment calls against a disclosed rubric, not disguised as a hard figure; the specific source and a confidence rating behind every country's score live in the project's curated data files.

ScoreUS Export-Control Access Tier

As of this research pass, only the UAE (4) and Saudi Arabia (3) have any disclosed bilateral arrangement at all, both dated within the last year (UAE's BIS Country Group A:5 upgrade, July 2026; Saudi Arabia's HUMAIN/G42 chip authorization, Nov 2025). This space moves fast — the prior "AI Diffusion Rule," which would have imposed a worldwide license requirement, was rescinded in May 2025 in favor of exactly this kind of bilateral dealmaking — so this factor should be re-verified before any external presentation of this project rather than treated as static.

6. Missing-Data Handling

A country missing one of the three US Integration Depth inputs still gets a score — the weighted average renormalizes over whichever factors are available, rather than treating a missing input as a zero. A country with zero available inputs for an axis shows N/A for that axis and for the derived Net Alignment Score, and renders gray on the Overview map rather than silently defaulting to a floor score. This mirrors exactly how the companion MENASA Risk Monitor handles Iran's sanctions-driven data gaps.

Investment and compute figures are undercounts by design: only deals attributable to a specific country with a specific dollar or megawatt figure and a citable source are scored. Several of the 17 countries show N/A on these two factors not because nothing is happening, but because nothing at that level of specificity was found in this research pass — headline ambition figures without a dated, sourced dollar amount are recorded for context but explicitly excluded from the score.

8. Additional Features

Beyond the composite score, the dashboard pairs four layers designed to keep the alignment score grounded in checkable events rather than standing alone as an unexplained number:

  • Policy-event tracker — a chronological, sourced feed of chip-policy events (the AI Diffusion Rule's issuance and rescission, the Chip Security Act, the late-2025 Saudi/UAE chip authorizations, smuggling indictments, the UAE's Country Group upgrade) with links to how each connects to the scoring model.
  • Auto-generated country briefs — a Bottom-Line-Up-Front and Key Judgments per country, downloadable as PDF. These are templated from the underlying cited data, not free-generated by an LLM at runtime — deterministic, so the brief always matches the current data and every sentence traces back to a specific cited row.
  • Scenario Lab — live reweighting of the US Integration Depth sub-weights and the US-vs-China axis balance, with 5 named presets each carrying a stated rationale (e.g. an "export-control-centric" preset weights BIS status at 70%, on the reasoning that a regulatory label may be stickier than capital commitments). Also includes a Normalization Sensitivity check (recomputes the ranking against alternative ceiling values) and a Rank Stability analysis (samples many weight configurations and reports how much each country's rank actually moves) — never a claim that any one configuration is the "correct" one.
  • Overview map metric selector — switches the choropleth between Net Alignment, US Integration Depth, China Exposure Depth, disclosed AI investment, disclosed compute capacity, and AI governance maturity, each with its own color scale.

9. Limitations

  • China Exposure Depth is a single-factor axis, resting entirely on Chinese tech penetration (Huawei). A stronger version would add a second, independent China-tie factor if and when comparably well-sourced data becomes available.
  • Confidence varies by country and factor: Qatar, Bahrain, Kuwait, and Oman's export-control tier remains Low confidence (a direct BIS source check was blocked; the working inference is documented but not independently verified), as is Turkey's. The 9 later-added regional countries inherit the same access gap, so their export-control-tier rows are marked Medium or Low confidence rather than silently upgraded.
  • Two scores rest on individual judgment calls worth a reader's attention: Israel's export-control tier rests on a single outlet's characterization of a Dec 2025 declaration rather than a confirmed regulatory text change, and Egypt's China-exposure and export-control scores are the single most likely to move in the next research pass given an unresolved, live Huawei-vs-US-backed bid for its national AI data center.
  • This is a fast-moving policy space — the two BIS decisions the export-control tier is built around (UAE, Saudi Arabia) are both less than a year old at time of writing, and the framework itself only stabilized after a May 2025 rule rescission. The export-control tier and the policy-event tracker are the two most time-sensitive parts of this project.
  • The tracker's country set expanded well beyond its original 2-country design (see Coverage above); later additions may have shallower historical grounding than the states covered from the start.
  • A single Net Alignment Score cannot fully capture a country simultaneously deepening ties with both the US and China in different sectors — the policy-event tracker exists precisely to surface that nuance alongside the composite number.
  • The choropleth map uses simplified public-domain boundaries — fine for color-by-country comparison at a regional zoom level, not suitable for any use requiring precise or politically sensitive boundary accuracy.
  • This is a comparative screening and monitoring tool, not investment advice or a prediction of government policy.
