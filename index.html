Background

About

Muhammad Rafay Waqar is a policy analyst and researcher working at the intersection of geopolitical risk, macroeconomics, and quantitative data analytics across emerging markets. He translates qualitative regional research into structured, data-driven risk assessments that help organisations navigate global volatility.

He holds a B.A. in Political Science with a Minor in Technology Management from the University of California, Davis (Class of 2026). His analytical foundation was built at the highest levels of multilateral finance in Washington, D.C. At the International Monetary Fund, he conducted macroeconomic and financial policy analysis for the OEDMD constituency, which represents Algeria, Ghana, Iran, Libya, Morocco, Pakistan, and Tunisia on the IMF Executive Board, working under Executive Director Bahador Bijani and Senior Advisor Saifullah Dogar. At the World Bank Group, he supported the Executive Director's office across work programs spanning 8 MENA countries, contributing to country-level programmes and the Pakistan Country Strategy Framework 2024. These institutional roles sharpened his ability to assess how sovereign debt vulnerabilities, trade policy, and macroeconomic pressures interact under real political constraints across the Middle East, North Africa, and South Asia.

He complements this macroeconomic expertise with strong technical capabilities, using Python, R, SQL, Stata, Tableau, and Power BI to transform complex datasets into actionable insights. This is reinforced by a multilingual profile spanning English, Urdu, Punjabi, Hindi, French, and Persian, enabling direct analysis of primary regional sources and regulatory developments across the MENA and South Asia corridors without relying on translation.

A published analytical writer since 2018, he has authored 12+ op-eds and features across The News International, The Express Tribune, and Dawn, examining global food security, Pakistan's water crisis, the Uyghur question, Gulf post-oil economics, and the broader dynamics of political economy and international affairs. He has also contributed to policy initiatives on the circular economy, Extended Producer Responsibility, and the emerging UN Global Plastics Treaty, helping identify partnership and policy opportunities for one of Pakistan's leading recycling alliances.

Beyond institutions, Rafay has demonstrated leadership through civic impact. He founded Water4Pakistan, crowdfunding $5,000 to deliver clean drinking water to children in rural Punjab, and served as elected International Students Representative at ASUCD, UC Davis's student government, advocating for over 6,000 international students and securing institutional partnerships across campus.

He is currently building a public research portfolio focused on Pakistan's macroeconomic vulnerabilities, MENA geopolitical risk, and AI governance, with a particular interest in how Gulf states and emerging markets are navigating the US-China technology competition and what state-directed AI development means for regional stability. He is actively seeking opportunities in strategy, economic and geopolitical risk consulting, business intelligence, and international development, where he can help organisations navigate uncertainty and make informed strategic decisions.
