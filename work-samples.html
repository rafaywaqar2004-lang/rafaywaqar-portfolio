Memo 01 — Export Compliance & Trade Controls Gulf AI Tracker

Diversion & Re-Export Risk of a Proposed HUMAIN-Adjacent Compute Deployment

To Head of Export Compliance & Trade Controls, [US AI Hardware/Cloud Infrastructure Firm]
From Muhammad Rafay Waqar
Date September 2026
Bottom Line Up Front

A proposed compute or partnership deal with HUMAIN or a HUMAIN-adjacent Saudi entity carries moderate, not high, diversion risk — but the compliance posture required to keep it that way is narrower than it looks. Saudi Arabia's chip access runs through a single, named, capped authorization (HUMAIN, ≤35,000 GB300-class-accelerator equivalents, security and reporting conditions attached), not a country-wide rule like the UAE's July 2026 upgrade. The one live item worth flagging before signature is Saudi-linked connectivity infrastructure now extending into Syria, a jurisdiction that remains license-required for advanced-computing items regardless of the broader 2025 sanctions relief there.

Key Judgments

  • Saudi Arabia's chip authorization is real but structurally narrower than the UAE's. On 19 Nov 2025, Commerce authorized HUMAIN to purchase up to the equivalent of 35,000 Nvidia GB300-class accelerators, subject to security and reporting conditions — an entity-specific carve-out, not a Country Group upgrade. Saudi Arabia sits at this project's tier 3 of 5; the UAE was separately upgraded to tier 4 in July 2026 via a named Country Group change covering G42, Core42, and eight named US firms. (High confidence — both primary-sourced to BIS/Commerce actions.)
  • A deal's compliance exposure is concentrated in one counterparty, which cuts both ways. Because the authorization is entity-specific to HUMAIN rather than country-wide, due diligence should center on HUMAIN's own reporting and security-condition compliance record — Saudi Arabia's general trade-control status (Country Group D:3/D:4, standard licensing) hasn't changed and isn't the operative constraint. (High confidence on the mechanism.)
  • No active sanctions-evasion or secondary-sanctions signal is on file for Saudi Arabia specifically. The only listed OFAC action (Global Magnitsky designations tied to the 2018 Khashoggi killing) targets individuals on human-rights grounds, not export-control evasion. (Medium confidence — re-verify against OFAC's SDN list before signature.)
  • The one live watch item: Saudi capital is funding connectivity infrastructure into Syria, a jurisdiction still license-required for chips. In Feb 2026, Saudi Telecom Company and Syria's Sovereign Fund signed a ~$800m joint venture ("SilkLink") building a 4,500km fiber network and a Mediterranean cable landing station at Tartus — explicitly framed by Saudi officials as extending HUMAIN's AI-hub connectivity toward Europe. Syria remains Country Group D:5 for advanced-computing items specifically, even after its broader 2025 sanctions relief. (Moderate confidence — worth a deal-specific contractual carve-out.)
Recommendation

Proceed with standard entity-level due diligence on HUMAIN directly — its BIS reporting-condition compliance history, not Saudi Arabia's country status, is the actual risk driver. Add one deal-specific term: written confirmation that no compute capacity under this agreement is routed to, or accessible from, Syria-based infrastructure. Re-screen HUMAIN and named counterparties against OFAC's SDN list immediately before signature rather than relying on this September 2026 research pass.

Built entirely from this project's own already-cited, sourced data: data/curated/export_control_tier.csv, sanctions_data.csv, and ai_investment_deals.csv in the Gulf AI & Tech-Bloc Alignment Tracker. Memo 02 — EM Sovereign Debt / Macro Strategy EM Macro Engine

Should This Desk's Process Incorporate the EM Macro Engine's Panel AR(1) Forecasts?

To Portfolio Manager, EM Sovereign Debt / Macro Strategy Desk
From Muhammad Rafay Waqar
Date September 2026
Bottom Line Up Front

Yes for growth, as one input among several — no for inflation, as currently specified. A panel AR(1) model refit annually on an expanding window and tested out-of-sample across 10 real years (2016–2025) modestly beats a naive "assume no change" baseline on GDP growth (4.36 vs. 4.91 MAE, pooled across 307 country-years) but only wins in 5 of those 10 individual years. On inflation, the same framework loses to the naive baseline pooled (9.74 vs. 11.05 MAE) despite a statistically significant persistence coefficient (0.574, p<0.0001) — real, just not enough to beat "nothing changed."

Key Judgments

  • The growth signal is real but modest — treat it as a tilt, not a forecast. A 0.55 MAE improvement pooled over 307 country-years is genuine and walk-forward-validated, but winning only 5 of 10 individual years means no single year's forecast should be trusted in isolation. (High confidence in the numbers; moderate confidence in how much would survive live trading costs, which this backtest doesn't model.)
  • The inflation output should not be used as a forecast in its current form. The model loses to a naive random walk on pooled MAE — the single most decision-relevant fact for a desk evaluating adoption, disclosed prominently rather than buried. (High confidence.)
  • A single-year backtest would have told the opposite, wrong story on both variables. The original 2024-only snapshot showed a 25% model win on inflation (10.63 vs. 14.12 MAE) — the 10-year walk-forward result reverses that. A textbook illustration of why single-period backtests overstate confidence in EM macro forecasting. (High confidence — a disclosed, documented correction within the project itself.)
  • Adding global macro drivers (oil, US rates, VIX, DXY) improves in-sample fit but doesn't close the out-of-sample gap for growth. In-sample R² roughly doubles (0.06→0.12), but the driver-augmented specification still loses to naive out-of-sample (3.29 MAE). The current specification looks closer to its useful ceiling than a driver-engineering exercise would fix. (Moderate confidence — one specification test, not an exhaustive search.)
Recommendation

Incorporate the growth forecast as one directional input into a broader EM growth view — never standalone, given the 5-of-10-years win rate. Do not incorporate the inflation forecast as-is; if inflation forecasting from this framework matters, the next step is a genuinely different specification, not re-tuning the current one. Hold any third-party EM forecasting tool to the same walk-forward standard before adopting it — refit-and-test on an expanding window across many real years, never one split.

Built entirely from this project's own already-computed, disclosed backtest output: forecast-module/rolling_backtest.py and rolling_backtest_results.csv (307/298 country-year observations, 2016–2025) in the EM Macro & Geopolitical Risk Engine.
